DESIGN GOAL

My goal for this project was to bring a mix of stealth, action, and investigation. A believable story with emotional stakes like Splinter Cell and Metal Gear Solid 1, combined with narrative-driven spatial progression. I wanted every space to feel purposeful — not just visually distinct, but emotionally and mechanically meaningful.

The level is structured around a rising emotional arc:

Vulnerability → Tension → Loss → Hope → Empowerment

My core design goals focused on three interconnected pillars:

Use Environmental Storytelling to drive narrative progression
Provide Multiple Infiltration Routes to support player agency
Employ Escalating Spatial Hierarchy to reinforce emotional beats

LEVEL OVERVIEW & HIGHLIGHTS

Setting & Tone

Setting: A remote forest concealing a clandestine enemy research facility

Tone & Mood: Isolation, tension, dread, rising urgency

Engine: UE5 Third-Person Shooter PC

Synopsis

This level is set in a remote forest where a spec-ops squad is ambushed while approaching a hidden enemy research facility. The player — the sole survivor of the crash — must navigate hostile territory, uncover the truth behind the ambush, and sabotage the facility before the looming danger begins to surface.

NARRATIVE-DRIVEN ENVIRONMENT STORYTELLING

What I designed

  • Used environmental storytelling to create a world that feels alive and reflects the enemy facility's current state.
  • Mixed in small storytelling details throughout the environment — overheard enemy chatter, posters, warning signs, and a cracked mirror — to give the world personality and let the narrative unfold naturally.
  • Used environmental breadcrumbs — blood trails, stone debris, tree branches, bodies — to deepen immersion.

  • Positioned meshes and props to subtly guide the player toward revealing secrets about the facility — lighting contrasts and visual gaps invite exploration.

ENEMY TYPES OVERVIEW

Watch Video

To support both narrative progression and stealth-focused gameplay, the facility features a coordinated enemy ecosystem. Any guard who detects the player immediately alerts the entire team, creating high-stakes tension and encouraging careful navigation.

Enemy Type 01

Standby Guards

Role: Static sentinels controlling key choke points. Remain stationed at fixed positions, maintaining constant line of sight over important doors and corridors.

Encourage stealth takedowns · Create predictable high-pressure obstacles · Reinforce the feeling of a tightly controlled facility.

Enemy Type 02

Patrolling Guards

Role: Mobile threats that sweep the environment. Move along predefined patrol routes, react quickly, and are designed to catch players who rely on predictable patterns.

Test player reflexes and timing · Add dynamism and unpredictability · Force players to track movement cycles.

Enemy Type 03

Enemy Workers

Role: Non-combat staff who still pose a detection risk. Unarmed but alert — they report suspicious activity to nearby guards and populate the environment to make it feel operational.

Challenge player awareness · Introduce low-threat, high-impact detection sources · Encourage players to consider non-combatants in their strategy.

SUPPORTS MULTIPLE PLAYSTYLES

Watch Video
Left Path — Ruins Building
Right Path — Rooftop Climb
Main Gate — Frontal Assault

Three Distinct Entry Paths

  • Left Path — Collapsed Building: A tense, grounded route through debris. Lift a fallen door and slip through a fractured fence. Quiet and gear-rich.
  • Right Path — Rooftop Climb: A stealthy vertical approach. Scale a box to reach the roof — exposed but silent. Ideal for precision and avoidance.
  • Main Gate — Frontal Assault: The boldest route. Straight through the front with patrols, cover, and chaos. Perfect for players who want to go loud.

Each path reflects a different mindset — precision, aggression, or survival — and sets the tone for what's coming inside the facility.

TECHNIQUES

Use Breadcrumbs to Guide Players

In this example, I used scattered stone debris as a natural breadcrumb trail to lead the player toward the hidden entrance.
Subtle blood traces on the floor guide attention toward the location of dead bodies, reinforcing the narrative and directing toward the next point of interest.
Fallen tree branches on the ground serve more than a decorative purpose — they guide the player toward the intended pathway leading into the facility.

Points of Interest (POI)

Amid the surrounding greenery, a barren tree naturally draws the player's eye, while the yellow building and the tower's blinking light serve as strong visual anchors that guide navigation.

Affordances

The flashing light toward the yellow edge highlights that the building is climbable. Since many games use yellow to mark traversal elements, I applied the same convention to ensure player guidance.
I applied the same visual-guidance technique in a small environmental puzzle, where the player must pull an object close to the wall and use it as a step to progress.

Landmarks

A central structure anchors the player's orientation and serves as a consistent landmark. No matter where the player is in the environment, this structure remains visible, helping them maintain spatial awareness and navigate the space more intuitively.

Environmental Storytelling

A small lab space marked by bloodstains, fallen bodies, and prisoner graffiti, conveying a sudden violent outbreak and the desperation of those trapped inside.
A cramped dorm with scattered papers and a broken mirror, using subtle environmental cues to show stress, declining morale, and psychological strain on personnel.
A sealed bunker entrance using warning signs, red lighting, and improvised barricades to suggest an emergency lockdown and hint at a dangerous event deeper inside.
A monitoring station with multi-screen displays and restricted-access messages, establishing the facility's hierarchy and surveillance culture.
A work zone decorated with dialogue snippets and "Project Dawn" labels, revealing and foreshadowing instability around the prototype.
A storage and control area featuring a shift-reminder poster and audio-log terminal, reinforcing rising security concerns and the presence of internal threats.

Contrast & Composition

Light, shadow, and color are used to emphasize important gameplay areas or objects.

Spatial Composition

Additional side paths are added to encourage exploration, offering loot or narrative elements.

Framing & Leading Lines

Highlighted stairs and directional edges form clear leading lines that communicate the intended movement path.
The corridor's converging lines frame the bright exit, naturally pulling the player toward the open space ahead.
Leading lines from the path, terrain, and fencing guide the player's eye directly toward the command-centre entrance.
The terrain slopes and tree placement frame the structure, creating a clear visual funnel that pulls the player toward the tower.

PROCESS

1

Research, Ideation & Planning

Goal
Develop a strong, fully formed level concept from the ground up.
Brainstorm board (Miro)
Concept Document
Map Flowchart

Approach:

  • Used Miro to explore a wide range of ideas across key categories: setting, locations, events, enemies, and mechanics
  • Had other level designers vote on their favourite concepts to highlight the most promising directions
  • Built an initial concept document (LDD draft) outlining the location vision, player objectives, and core gameplay loop
  • Collected visual references to establish the intended atmosphere, tone, and environmental style

Outcome: My mentor recommended breaking the overall idea into smaller, more manageable pieces as the original scope was too large. Based on that feedback, I divided the full level concept into three acts and chose to develop Act 1 first. Act 1 is built around three major distinct areas: Crash Site & Enemy Outpost, Perimeter Yard, and Facility Interior leading into the Bunkers.

3

First Roughout

Goal
Build a functional, testable version of the level as quickly as possible.
PureRef Board

Process:

  • Shifted the initial 2D layout into Unreal Engine 5 and constructed the environment using simple block-out geometry
  • Continuously iterated in play mode to assess navigation, sightlines, and gameplay flow
  • Implemented placeholder elements like enemy encounters, NPCs, and loot to simulate intended gameplay structure
  • Used a Level Design Kit for rapid prototyping and developed a PureRef board for visual direction

Result: The level reached a fully playable state early in development. Despite minimal visuals, the core gameplay loop — exploration, reward discovery, and story progression — was clearly communicated.

4

Iterating the Roughout

Goal
Transform the Crash Site and Perimeter Yard into a cohesive, immersive opening sequence.
Animation preview

Solution:

  • Redesigned the Crash Site to open with a short cinematic conversation, followed by a panoramic reveal and title card
  • Reworked the left-side perimeter entry: replaced a simple cut-down fence with a small environmental puzzle supported by breadcrumbs
  • Introduced an early jump challenge using a broken cliff edge that naturally leads players toward their first enemy encounter
  • Refined lighting and sky atmosphere to establish a cohesive mood and enhance sense of scale
  • Game Mode upgrade: switched to ALS for smoother movement and interaction

Result: The redesigned introduction now feels alive, intentional, and narratively cohesive. Players begin with a cinematic overview and naturally follow a guided path shaped by visual cues, environmental puzzles, and storytelling.

5

Moving to the Blockout

Goal
Assessed the level using core level design principles — identified spaces lacking spatial depth, player guidance, and purposeful interactions.
  • Improved player orientation by introducing a tall landmark structure with distinctively coloured pillars, creating a strong visual anchor
  • Identified and fixed a sequence break where players could miss key Commander Suite story beats
  • Expanded dormitory spaces into two distinct floors — Dorm A (golden path) and Dorm B (optional exploration)
  • Redesigned the Armory to improve pacing, combat readability, and tactical options through refined cover placement
  • Used consistent prefix-wing-depth codes (e.g., LAB K12, SEC K03) to create coherent world logic

Result: The updated blockout delivered stronger visual rhythm and clearer spatial hierarchy. The facility began to feel more alive, layered, and naturally navigable.

6

Iterating the Blockout

Goal
Finalise the first tutorial enemy encounter; polish the Right-Side areas and Left-Side Parking Lot; refine Briefing Suite and Commander Suite; begin lighting and post-processing.
  • Moved the first enemy encounter to the cliff edge to give the player a breather before the heavily guarded gate cinematic
  • Reworked the parking lot with stealth traversal props, attention-guiding lighting, and Blueprint interactions
  • Refined the Briefing Suite geometry, rebalanced lighting, and reorganised props for cleaner, more functional environment
  • Colour-coded non-interactive props in neutral grey to reduce visual noise
  • Introduced post-processing to reinforce a slightly oppressive mood and guide player perception

Result: The level became more readable, grounded, and tactically interesting. Lighting and visual cues helped guide navigation and reinforce the setting's mood.

7

Refining the Blockout

Goal
Deepen the player experience in the Armory Annex and Entrance to Security Command Center — improving pacing, verticality, and environmental storytelling.

  • Added a truck entering the facility as a dynamic visual anchor using motion and composition as leading lines toward the main entrance

KEY DESIGN LESSONS

  • Emotional pacing is as important as mechanical pacing.
  • Environmental storytelling can carry narrative weight without cutscenes.
  • Spatial hierarchy naturally guides player expectations.
  • Every area should answer "Why does this exist?"

This project helped me refine my ability to blend narrative and gameplay into a cohesive experience.
