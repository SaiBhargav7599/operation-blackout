01

Design Goal

My goal for this project was to bring a mix of stealth, action, and investigation — a believable story with emotional stakes like Splinter Cell and Metal Gear Solid 1, combined with narrative-driven spatial progression.

I wanted every space to feel purposeful — not just visually distinct, but emotionally and mechanically meaningful. The level is structured around a rising emotional arc:

Vulnerability → Tension → Loss → Hope → Empowerment

My core design goals focused on three interconnected pillars:

→ Use Environmental Storytelling to drive narrative progression
→ Provide Multiple Infiltration Routes to support player agency
→ Employ Escalating Spatial Hierarchy to reinforce emotional beats

03

Level Overview & Highlights

Setting & Tone

Setting: A remote forest concealing a clandestine enemy research facility

Tone & Mood: Isolation, tension, dread, rising urgency

Engine: UE5 Third-Person Shooter PC

Synopsis

A spec-ops squad is ambushed while approaching a hidden enemy research facility. The player — the sole survivor — must navigate hostile territory, uncover the truth, and sabotage the facility before the looming danger surfaces.

ACT 1 — Mission Objectives

Narrative-Driven Environment Storytelling

Video Overview
1 / 5

Every space should tell a story without a single line of dialogue.

  • Used environmental storytelling to make the facility feel alive and reflect its current state.
  • Mixed in overheard enemy chatter, posters, warning signs, and a cracked mirror to let the narrative unfold naturally as the player explores.
  • Used environmental breadcrumbs — blood trails, stone debris, tree branches, bodies — to deepen immersion.

Rewarding Exploration

Video Overview
1 / 4

Exploration must feel rewarded — both narratively and mechanically.

  • Placed hidden and visible rewards throughout to drive exploration and give a sense of meaningful discovery.
  • 8 Interaction triggers  ·  4 Audio logs  ·  4 Ammo & 8 Medical bandage pickups
  • Positioned props to subtly guide players toward revealing secrets — lighting contrasts and visual gaps invite exploration.

Enemy Types Overview

Video Overview

Any guard who detects the player immediately alerts the entire team — creating high-stakes tension and encouraging careful navigation.

Type 01

Standby Guards

Role: Static sentinels at key choke points. Maintain constant line of sight over important doors and corridors.

Encourage stealth takedowns · Reinforce a tightly controlled facility feel.

Type 02

Patrolling Guards

Role: Mobile threats along predefined patrol routes. Catch players who rely on predictable patterns.

Add dynamism · Force players to track movement cycles.

Type 03

Enemy Workers

Role: Non-combat staff who still pose a detection risk. Unarmed but alert — report suspicious activity.

Challenge awareness · Make the environment feel operational.

Supports Multiple Playstyles

Video Overview
Left Path — Ruins Building
Right Path — Rooftop Climb
Main Gate — Frontal Assault

Each path reflects a different mindset — precision, aggression, or survival.

  • Left Path — Collapsed Building: Tense, grounded route through debris. Lift a fallen door and slip through a fractured fence.
  • Right Path — Rooftop Climb: Stealthy vertical approach — high exposure, low noise. Ideal for precision and avoidance.
  • Main Gate — Frontal Assault: Straight through with patrols, cover, and chaos. For players who want to go loud.

Techniques

Level design tools and methods applied throughout the project.

Use Breadcrumbs to Guide Players

Scattered stone debris as a natural breadcrumb trail leading the player toward the hidden entrance.
Subtle blood traces on the floor guide attention toward the location of dead bodies and the next point of interest.
Fallen tree branches guide the player toward the intended pathway leading into the facility.

Points of Interest (POI)

A barren tree draws the player's eye amid surrounding greenery, while the yellow building and tower's blinking light serve as strong visual anchors.

Affordances

The flashing light toward the yellow edge communicates the building is climbable — using the same yellow convention many games use for traversal.
Same visual-guidance technique applied to a small environmental puzzle — pull the object close and use it as a step to progress.

Research, Ideation & Planning

Goal
Develop a strong, fully formed level concept from the ground up.
Brainstorm board (Miro)
Concept Document
Map Flowchart
  • Used Miro to explore ideas across key categories: setting, locations, events, enemies, and mechanics
  • Had other level designers vote on favourite concepts to highlight the most promising directions
  • Built an initial concept document outlining location vision, player objectives, and core gameplay loop
  • Collected visual references to establish intended atmosphere, tone, and environmental style

Outcome: My mentor recommended breaking the concept into smaller pieces. I divided the full level into three acts and focused on Act 1 first — built around three major areas: Crash Site & Enemy Outpost, Perimeter Yard, and Facility Interior leading into the Bunkers.

First Roughout

Goal
Build a functional, testable version of the level as quickly as possible.
PureRef Board
1 / 4
  • Shifted 2D layout into UE5 and constructed environment using simple block-out geometry
  • Iterated in play mode to assess navigation, sightlines, and gameplay flow
  • Implemented placeholder enemies, NPCs, and loot to simulate intended gameplay structure

Result: The level reached a fully playable state early. Core gameplay loop — exploration, reward discovery, story progression — was clearly communicated despite minimal visuals.

4

Iterating the Roughout

Goal
Transform the Crash Site and Perimeter Yard into a cohesive, immersive opening sequence.
  • Redesigned Crash Site to open with a short cinematic, panoramic reveal, and title card
  • Replaced cut-down fence with a small environmental puzzle supported by breadcrumbs
  • Introduced an early jump challenge using a broken cliff edge leading to the first enemy encounter
  • Refined lighting and sky atmosphere · Switched Game Mode to ALS

Result: The introduction now feels alive, intentional, and narratively cohesive.

5

Moving to the Blockout

Goal
Address spaces lacking spatial depth, player guidance, and purposeful interactions.
  • Introduced a tall landmark structure with distinctively coloured pillars as a strong visual anchor
  • Fixed a sequence break where players could miss key Commander Suite story beats
  • Expanded dormitory into two floors — Dorm A (golden path) and Dorm B (optional exploration)
  • Redesigned the Armory for improved pacing, combat readability, and cover placement

Key Design Lessons

  • Emotional pacing is as important as mechanical pacing.
  • Environmental storytelling can carry narrative weight without cutscenes.
  • Spatial hierarchy naturally guides player expectations.
  • Every area should answer "Why does this exist?"

This project helped me refine my ability to blend narrative and gameplay into a cohesive experience.
