01

Design Goal

My goal for this project was to bring a mix of stealth, action, and investigation. A believable story with emotional stakes like Splinter Cell and Metal Gear Solid 1, combined with narrative-driven spatial progression. I wanted every space to feel purposeful not just visually distinct, but emotionally and mechanically meaningful. The level is structured around a rising emotional arc:

Vulnerability → Tension → Loss → Hope → Empowerment

My core design goals focused on three interconnected pillars:

Use Environmental storytelling to drive narrative progression
Provide Multiple infiltration routes to support player agency
Employ Escalating spatial hierarchy to reinforce emotional beats

Level Overview & Highlights

Setting & Tone

Setting: A remote forest concealing a clandestine enemy research facility

Tone & Mood: Isolation, tension, dread, rising urgency

Constraints:

Engine: Unreal Engine 5  ·  Perspective: TPS (third person shooter)  ·  Platform: PC

Synopsis

The level is set in a remote forest where a special-ops squad is ambushed while approaching a hidden enemy research facility. The player, the sole survivor of the crash, must navigate hostile territory, uncover the truth behind the ambush, and sabotage the facility before the looming danger begins to surface.

Narrative-Driven Environment Storytelling

Every space should tell a story without a single line of dialogue.

Enemy Types Overview

Video Overview

To support both narrative progression and stealth-focused gameplay, the facility features a coordinated enemy ecosystem. Any guard who detects the player immediately alerts the entire team, creating high-stakes tension and encouraging careful navigation.

Type 01

Standby Guards

Role: Static sentinels controlling key choke points.

Behaviour: Remain stationed at fixed positions. Maintain a constant line of sight over important doors, corridors, or objectives.

Design Purpose: Encourage players to engage with stealth takedowns · Create predictable but high-pressure obstacles · Reinforce the feeling of infiltrating a tightly controlled facility.

Type 02

Patrolling Guards

Role: Mobile threats that sweep the environment.

Behaviour: Move along predefined patrol routes. React quickly. Designed to catch players who rely too heavily on predictable patterns.

Design Purpose: Test player reflexes and timing · Add dynamism and unpredictability to stealth sections · Increase tension by forcing players to track movement cycles.

Type 03

Enemy Workers

Role: Non-combat staff who still pose a detection risk.

Behaviour: Unarmed but alert. Report suspicious activity to nearby guards. Populate the environment to make it feel alive and operational.

Design Purpose: Challenge player awareness and environmental scanning · Introduce low-threat but high-impact detection sources · Encourage players to consider non-combatants in their stealth strategy.

Supports Multiple Playstyles

Video Overview
Left Path – Collapsed Building
Right Path – Rooftop Climb
Main Gate – Frontal Assault

Designed multiple areas inside the facility supporting multiple playstyles — stealth, direct combat & silent takedowns.

I designed three distinct entry paths to support different playstyles and emotional tones:

  • Left Path – Collapsed Building: A tense, grounded route through debris. You lift a fallen door and slip inside through a fractured fence. It's quiet and gear rich.
  • Right Path – Rooftop Climb: A stealthy vertical approach. Players scale a box to reach the roof, exposed but silent. Ideal for those who favour precision and avoidance.
  • Main Gate – Frontal Assault: The boldest route. Straight through the front, with patrols, cover, and chaos. Perfect for players who want to take control and go loud but not easy.

Each path is designed to reflect a different mindset — stealth, survival, or aggression and to set the tone for what's coming inside the facility. Added alternate paths, climbable spots, and visual guides (lights, silhouettes) to encourage creative thinking and reward players for outsmarting enemies.

Techniques

Level design tools and methods applied throughout the project.

Use Breadcrumbs to Guide Players

In this example, I used scattered stone debris as a natural breadcrumb trail to lead the player toward the hidden entrance.
As the player steps the floor, subtle blood traces on the floor guide their attention toward the location of the dead bodies, reinforcing the narrative and directing them toward the next point of interest.
The fallen tree branches on the ground serve more than a decorative purpose, they guide the player toward the intended pathway leading into the facility.

Points of Interest (POI)

Amid the surrounding greenery, a barren tree naturally draws the player's eye, while the yellow building and the tower's blinking light serve as strong visual anchors that consistently attract attention and guide player navigation.

Affordances

The flashing light towards the yellow edge highlights acts as a clear visual cue that the building is climbable. Since many games use yellow to mark traversal elements, I applied the same convention to ensure player guidance.
I applied the same visual-guidance technique in a small environmental puzzle, where the player must pull an object close to the wall and use it as a step to progress.

Landmarks

1 / 7

A central structure anchors the player's orientation and serves as a consistent landmark. No matter where the player is in the environment, this structure remains visible, helping them maintain spatial awareness and navigate the space more intuitively.

Environmental Storytelling

A small lab space marked by bloodstains, fallen bodies, and prisoner graffiti, conveying a sudden violent outbreak and the desperation of those trapped inside the facility.
A cramped dorm with scattered papers and a broken mirror, using subtle environmental cues to show stress, declining morale, and the psychological strain on personnel.
A sealed bunker entrance using warning signs, red lighting, and improvised barricades to suggest an emergency lockdown and hint at a dangerous event deeper inside the facility.
A monitoring station with multi-screen displays and restricted-access messages, establishing the facility's hierarchy, surveillance culture, and compartmentalized structure.
A work zone decorated with dialogue snippets and a "Project Dawn" labels, revealing and foreshadowing instability around the prototype.
A storage and control area featuring a shift-reminder poster and audio-log terminal, reinforcing rising security concerns and the presence of internal threats.

Research, Ideation and Planning

Goal
Develop a strong, fully formed level concept from the ground up.
Ideas on Miro
Concept Document

Approach:

  • Used Miro to explore a wide range of ideas across key categories: setting, locations, events, enemies, and mechanics — see the board on the right.
  • Had other level designers vote on their favourite concepts to highlight the most promising directions.
  • Built an initial concept document (early LDD draft) outlining the location vision, player objectives, and the core gameplay loop.
  • Collected visual references to establish the intended atmosphere, tone, and environmental style.

Outcome: After reviewing my concept document, my mentor recommended breaking the overall idea into smaller, more manageable pieces, as the original scope was too large. Based on that feedback, I divided the full level concept into three acts and chose to focus on developing Act 1 first, applying level-design fundamentals with proper structure and clarity.

Act 1 is built around three major, distinct areas: Crash Site & Enemy Outpost, Perimeter Yard, and Facility Interior leading into the Bunkers. To support this refined direction, I created a detailed map flowchart for Act 1, outlining player progression, spatial transitions, and key gameplay beats.

First Roughout — Establishing the Playable Foundation

Goal
Build a functional, testable version of the level as quickly as possible.
PureRef board
1 / 4

Process:

  • Shifted the initial 2D layout into Unreal Engine 5 and constructed the environment using simple block out geometry.
  • Continuously iterated in play mode to assess navigation, sightlines, and overall gameplay flow.

4

Iterating the Roughout

Goal
Transform the Crash Site and Perimeter Yard into a cohesive, immersive opening sequence. Playtests showed that players often missed key elements like the crash, the initial conversation trigger, and the cliff-edge panorama — resulting in a weak narrative start. The redesign also replaces the simple cut-down fence with a small environmental puzzle supported by breadcrumbs, adding player agency and reinforcing the facility's security. Overall, the goal was to strengthen environmental storytelling, guide early player progression, and elevate the visual and emotional tone from the very first moments of gameplay.

Solution:

  • Redesigned the Crash site to open with a short cinematic conversation, followed by a panoramic reveal and title card.
  • Reworked the left-side perimeter entry: instead of a simple cut-down fence, the route now features a small environmental puzzle supported by breadcrumbs. This adds a moment of player agency, slows the pacing in a meaningful way, and reinforces the idea that the facility is secure and must be outsmarted rather than simply walked into.
  • Introduced an early jump challenge using a broken cliff edge that naturally leads players toward their first enemy encounter, creating tension and engagement right from the start.
  • Refined lighting, and sky atmosphere to establish a cohesive mood and enhance the sense of scale and realism.
  • Game Mode upgrade: switched to ALS for smoother movement and interaction.
Result

The redesigned introduction now feels alive, intentional, and narratively cohesive. Players begin with a cinematic overview, transition into a moment of tension, and naturally follow a guided path shaped by visual cues, environmental puzzles, and storytelling. The improved atmosphere, exploration opportunities, and refined gameplay systems create a stronger first impression and a more engaging start to the experience.

5

Moving to the Blockout

Goal
I assessed the level using core level design principles and identified that the spaces of the facility lacked spatial depth, player guidance, and purposeful Interactions.

Solution:

  • Improved player orientation by introducing a tall landmark structure with distinctively coloured structured pillars, creating a strong visual anchor that guides navigation. This landmark naturally draws players toward a key narrative reveal connected to the SEC K04 bunker.
  • Identified a sequence break where players could enter through SEC K03 and miss key Commander Suite story beats. To fix this, I locked SEC K03 and redesigned the traversal flow to guide players naturally toward the Commander Suites, ensuring consistent narrative delivery while preserving player agency.
  • Expanding Dormitory Spaces for Balanced Golden Path & Optional Exploration: Initially, the dorm building served as an optional exploration area with light rewards. After playtesting, I expanded the dorms into two distinct floors. Dorm A became part of the golden path, supporting core intel-gathering and narrative progression. Dorm B (Second Floor) was developed as an optional reward space, offering additional loot and traversal-based exploration opportunities. This adjustment created a healthier balance between required progression and optional discovery.
  • Redesigned the Armory to expand its interior layout, improving pacing, combat readability, and tactical options through refined cover placement. Introduced a locked SEC K02 bunker door to subtly foreshadow deeper facility routes, and rebuilt a key narrative moment in the Armory Annex to strengthen story delivery during player exploration.
  • Each area uses a consistent prefix-wing-depth code (e.g., LAB K12, SEC K03) to create a coherent world logic that supports player orientation and environmental storytelling.
Result

The updated blockout layout delivered a stronger visual rhythm and a clearer spatial hierarchy, allowing players to read the environment more intuitively. The facility as a whole began to feel more alive, layered, and naturally navigable.

6

Iterating the Blockout

The Primary focus of this iteration was to:
  • Finalize the first tutorial enemy encounter.
  • Polish the Right-Side areas, including the Armory Annex and Commander Suite, as well as the Left-Side Parking Lot, improving layout clarity, navigation flow, and environmental storytelling.
  • Refine the Briefing Suite and Commander Suite, focusing on their connections from all entrance points to strengthen spatial hierarchy, and player orientation.
  • Start working on lighting and post-processing to enhance overall atmosphere, improve visual hierarchy, and support gameplay readability throughout the level.

Solution:

  • I originally planned the first enemy encounter right after the player descends the cliff, followed by a takedown and a cinematic showing the heavily guarded main gate. It felt like too much at once, so I moved the first encounter to the cliff edge to give the player a breather.
  • Reworked the parking lot with props for stealth traversal, attention-guiding lighting, and Blueprints for interactions.
  • In the Briefing Suite, my focus was on elevating clarity and purpose. I refined the geometry, rebalanced the lighting, and reorganized props to create a cleaner, more functional environment that feels lived-in and supports both player interaction and key narrative moments. All entrance points on the ground floor were also reworked to provide balanced combat encounters, ensuring fair and readable engagements from every approach.
  • Color-coded non-interactive props (e.g. doors) in neutral grey to reduce visual noise.
  • Introduced post-processing to reinforce a slightly oppressive mood and guide player perception.
Result

The level became more readable, grounded, and tactically interesting. The updated layout offered players more strategic options during combat, while lighting and visual cues helped guide navigation and reinforced the setting's mood.

7

Refining the Blockout

Goal
This iteration focused on deepening the player experience in the Armory Annex and Entrance to Security Command Center areas by: improving pacing and route design, adding verticality and strategic options, and expanding environmental storytelling and mood through lighting and narrative setups.
Cinematic truck entry
Animation preview
Animation preview

Solution:

  • Added dynamic environmental storytelling. A truck entering the facility becomes a visual anchor that draws the player's eye as they slide down the cliff, using motion and composition as leading lines toward the main entrance.
  • Expanded the Armory annex, blocking direct access & allowing player to foreshadowing of the scene, enemies & different approaches.
  • Added narrative graffiti and environmental storytelling elements to convey the area's past and hint at what occurred here, enriching player context and deepening the atmosphere.
  • Players were struggling to locate the ladder leading into the Security Command Center, so I replaced it with a rounded staircase supported by environmental leading lines and targeted lighting. This redesign creates a clearer visual path and naturally guides players toward the intended entry point.
  • As the player approaches the facility via the left-side route, the container corridor features a small, broken entry point that subtly foreshadows the storage complex hidden beneath the Security Command Center. A brief cinematic beat provides the player's first glimpse into the facility's deeper operations, ensuring that both infiltration paths deliver balanced narrative setup and consistent environmental storytelling.
Result

The dynamic truck-entry set piece that preserves player agency while supporting multiple approach options with narrative elements. The Armory Annex offered a more atmospheric, story-driven space, reinforcing the mystery of the facility and giving space for emotional pacing between combat sequences.

Key Design Lessons

Building this level taught me several important lessons:

  • Emotional pacing is as important as mechanical pacing.
  • Environmental storytelling can carry narrative weight without cutscenes.
  • Spatial hierarchy naturally guides player expectations.
  • Every area should answer "Why does this exist?"

This project helped me refine my ability to blend narrative and gameplay into a cohesive experience.
